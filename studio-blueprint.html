CAI 3801 Studio: a standalone, gamified AI & analytics platform

A research-backed plan for a course platform outside Canvas. Weekly lessons build named competencies, an AI tutor supports learning without doing the work, and grading pairs an AI first pass with a human decision. Built on Google Cloud.

The professor asked for eleven things. Each has a design decision below, the evidence behind it, and where it already runs in the prototype. Three findings shaped the design more than any others.

Guardrails decide whether AI tutors help

An unrestricted chatbot raised practice scores by 48% but cut exam scores by 17%. The same model with hints instead of answers removed the harm.9 A course-grounded tutor outperformed in-class active learning in a Harvard trial.8

Gamification helps, modestly, if it rewards mastery

Meta-analyses find small to medium positive effects (g ≈ 0.25 to 0.50).12 Rewards for completion alone can undermine motivation,6 and effects fade after about four weeks without a refresh.7

AI grading is uneven across tasks

Agreement with human graders ranges from near-human to poor, worst on analytic, judgment-heavy criteria.2021 So AI suggests, a TA decides, and each criterion must pass a calibration bar first.23

Ask 2Gamification that supports learning

What the evidence supports

  • Small to medium gains: cognitive g = .49, motivational g = .36, behavioral g = .25.1 Academic performance g = 0.50.2
  • Badges and performance graphs raise competence; teams and story raise relatedness. No element adds autonomy, so give students choices.5
  • Collaboration combined with friendly competition works best.1
  • Positive, specific feedback increases intrinsic motivation.6

What backfires

  • Rewards for showing up or finishing alone can undermine motivation (d ≈ −0.28 to −0.40).6
  • Effects drop after about four weeks, then partly recover.7
  • Leaderboards, points and badges are the elements most often linked to negative effects, including gaming the system.4

Rules for CAI 3801 Studio

  1. XP mostly for evidence of mastery (correct checks, rubric-scored work), less for opening a page. XP never counts toward the grade.
  2. The private competency radar and specific feedback are the main rewards.
  3. Team quests with an opt-in, team-level board; no ranked list of individuals.
  4. A light story: students are analysts at a fictional firm, and each lab is a client brief.
  5. Choice boards: two or three quest options a week.
  6. A "season" refresh around week 5 with new quest types.
  7. Streaks count weeks, not days, so a busy day never breaks one.

The prototype follows rule 1: opening a reading earns 10 XP, a correct check 40, graded work 100 to 150.

Ask 4Activity tracking and personalised next steps

  • Record events in a standard shape. Actor, verb, object, time, result, following xAPI (IEEE 9274.1.1) or 1EdTech Caliper 1.2, so the data stays portable.1011
  • Weight graded work above clicks. Across 17 courses, click data predicted poorly and added little once interim grades existed.12
  • Use transparent rules first. Overdue work, a weak competency backed by two graded items, five days inactive, repeated tutor questions without the check, and a strength worth stretching. Each shows the student why it appears.
  • Tie nudges to concrete course actions. Generic nudges showed no effect in large trials;24 course-embedded nudges linked to specific supports raised A/B grades by 4 points at Georgia State.25
  • Flags start a human conversation, never a penalty. Georgia State paired alerts with more advisors.13 Purdue's Course Signals claim did not survive scrutiny.26

Ask 11The AI tutor

  1. Hints and Socratic questions by default; never a full answer to graded work.927
  2. Grounded in course readings and instructor-written solutions, with citations.8
  3. Given a short summary of the student's recent attempts and gaps; Khan Academy saw a 6.1% gain in next-item correctness from this.28
  4. Judged by unaided performance (checks and quizzes without AI), because practice gains can hide exam losses.9
  5. Positioned as preparation for class, not a replacement.8

The prototype's tutor now runs retrieval-augmented generation in the browser: the readings are split into overlapping passages, ranked with BM25 and, when Ollama has an embedding model, with meaning-based search, merged by reciprocal rank fusion.808182 If nothing matches well it says the readings don't cover the question without calling the model. On 24 course questions the right reading was in the top three every time, with no wrong refusals; all 7 off-topic questions were declined. It has four modes (Explain, a three-step Hint ladder, Quiz me, Check me), cites numbered sources, flags numbers that are not in the sources, and switches to coaching when a student asks for graded answers. It also answers only from the readings, cites them by number, and refuses to give a student's own lab answers. On Google Cloud it would ground on a RAG Engine corpus of the syllabus, slides and readings.31

NewSeeing how students prompt, and helping them improve

The professor asked to see how students prompt AI and how they can get better. Three pieces in the Studio answer that.

  1. App Builder. Modelled on Stanford's Probability for AI, where students build a portfolio of apps with a code agent and must explain their reasoning live to a section leader.7071 Students describe an analytics app; the agent sees only the function name and the data, so the rules behind the tests must come from the student's prompt. A vague prompt fails the edge-case tests, the page names what was missing, and the next prompt fixes it. Every version is kept. When all tests pass the student explains how the app decides and what could break it, Piech's test of "growing alongside the AI".72 Apps run in a sandboxed frame with no access to the page or the network.
  2. My prompting (student). Every prompt is scored on six parts: Task, Context, References, Format, Rules and Iterate, adapted from Google's Prompting Essentials (Task, Context, References, Evaluate, Iterate).73 A coach picks the student's weakest recent prompt and rescores the rewrite as they type. Automated prompt assessment is an active research area with promising early results.7677
  3. Prompt insights (TA and faculty). Class trends by part, a "teach this next" card, the topics students ask the tutor about (themes rather than raw counts, as Toronto's tools summarise them), a searchable prompt gallery, each student's timeline and App Builder attempts, a CSV export, and an AI-drafted coaching note the TA edits before sending.7475 Teachers in a prompt-dashboard study found frequency charts hard to act on, so each view ends in a teaching action.75

Round two. Three more ideas from current research. Try first: for why and how questions the tutor asks for the student's guess before explaining, because the tutors that help most require an attempt before help, while a default answer-giving tutor lowered grades by 0.37 standard deviations in a 2,379-student trial.8384 You first, then AI, then compare in the Prompt Lab, after a business-course study of that reflective cycle.85 An AI-use log students download and submit with the final project, as in Lehigh's AI literacy course.86 Prompt insights also maps the class onto Anthropic's 4D AI Fluency framework: Delegation, Description, Discernment and Diligence.87

Round three. Two more, aimed at checking and remembering. Fact-check the AI: students label each sentence of an AI answer as true, false or can't tell from the data, then see why. It follows classroom "human validator" and claim-by-claim reality-check exercises, which reward careful checking over simply disagreeing with the AI.8889 Spaced quick review with a confidence check: earlier weeks come back on a Leitner schedule (a miss returns tomorrow, a confident right answer after up to 30 days), and students say how sure they are before the options show. An AI tutor built on spaced retrieval was linked to higher exam grades, though that study was not randomized; practice with feedback improves calibration; and errors made with high confidence are the ones people correct best.909192 Fact-check results now feed the Discernment row of the 4D card.

Rules: students are told their prompts are visible; scores coach and never grade, as in Texas State's non-punitive deployment.78 Questions to the tutor count only for intent (learning or answer-seeking), not the six-part score. Human contact still matters most: in Code in Place trials, 10 minutes with a person raised completion by 10 points, while chatbot access alone lowered it by 3.71 Even CS50's tutor, told not to, still gives code blocks up to a quarter of the time, so the course keeps a human check on learning.79

Why this shape

  • Agreement varies widely by task: near-human on checklist items,20 poor on analytic ones in a medical study.21
  • Bias against informal or non-native writing persists even with instructions not to.22
  • A human-in-the-loop math study kept mandatory verification and graded 23% faster.29

Calibration gate

  • Before AI suggestions are used on a criterion: quadratic weighted κ of at least 0.70 against human scores, less than 0.10 below human-to-human agreement, and a standardized mean difference under 0.15.23
  • Re-check weekly on a double-graded sample.
  • Analytic, judgment-heavy criteria stay human-led.

Policy: tell students where AI assisted, and give them a full human review with no penalty, as UNC and SDSU require.3233 UCF calls AI-only grading unethical.34 No USF-specific AI-grading policy was found; confirm with the department.

Ask 6MCP: course data for agents

  • Spec: MCP revision 2026-07-28 (stateless), stdio and Streamable HTTP transports, OAuth 2.1 for remote servers.4647
  • Course MCP server (built): seven tools: list_lessons, get_student_progress, recommend_next_steps, get_rubric, suggest_rubric_scores, record_review, class_overview. Students can read only their own data; grade-writing tools need a TA or faculty role.
  • Google-managed MCP servers: BigQuery and Firestore are generally available; Sheets is in developer preview.30 Host custom servers on Cloud Run behind IAM.48
  • Safety: keep a human able to deny tool calls, treat tool descriptions as untrusted, validate inputs, never pass tokens through.49 Model Armor screens prompt injection.30
  • Agent Skills: package course know-how (rubric grading, Socratic tutoring) as SKILL.md folders, an open standard now under the Linux Foundation and supported by Google's ADK.5051

Privacy and governance

  • Under FERPA, an outside service may receive student records only as a "school official" under the university's direct control.52 Student data should go only to university-contracted AI.
  • USF guidance says not to put protected student data into AI tools, and course chatbots should use USF-supported platforms.44 The Studio would need USF IT and data-governance approval before real students use it.
  • Recommendations and flags are readable rules; no raw risk score is shown to a student.
  • Students can see and correct their own records; AI-generated records are part of that.53

What exists today

Working prototype

Three personas, weekly quests, XP and badges, the skills radar, next steps with reasons, four practice tools (Prompt Lab, Tool Trainer, Agent Builder, Client meeting), the Discount Desk lab, the AI tutor, the grading queue with live AI suggestions on claude.ai, calibration, and model and MCP settings. Classmates' data is simulated.

Backend code (in the project folder)

A Cloud Run API, the course MCP server (seven tools, stdio and Streamable HTTP), one interface for Gemini, open models and OpenAI-compatible endpoints, the recommendation, competency and grading rules, and Terraform for Google Cloud. 67 automated tests pass locally, and role checks were confirmed by hand.

Not yet verified: real calls to Gemini, Firestore or BigQuery, the Terraform plan and the Docker builds. Nothing is deployed.
